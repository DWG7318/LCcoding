# LCCoding 3.1.0 Applicability and Service Topology Design

**Status:** Owner-approved design. The Owner delegated remaining design choices to the implementation process and does not require repeated clarification for ordinary details.

**Base:** LCCoding `3.0.0`, commit `64d4a7798442d0abdcee4b28ba5f39bad1bb024d`.

**Target:** LCCoding `3.1.0` method candidate. Release, global Skill deployment, and persistent BI installation are outside this design.

**Document role:** historical design provenance only. It does not change the current method or create project evidence. `SPEC.md` remains the sole semantic authority after an accepted implementation transfers these decisions into active clauses and mechanical contracts.

## 1. Problem statement

LCCoding 3.0 has two implicit assumptions that are no longer sufficient.

First, it can be read as a general engineering method even though its lifecycle depends on an identifiable product user, a complete business journey, real Workflow, an actor-facing product surface, Simulation, Backend/Core integration, and final journey acceptance. Applying the full method to a pure library, algorithm, driver, infrastructure component, one-off script, or headless internal service can force artificial UI, Simulation, or user-journey work that does not belong to the engineering object.

Second, the current lifecycle is still UI-centric. Product Formation already requires every realized Workflow capability to expose the same real capability through API and MCP contracts, but Real Product Integration proves a UI action and visible UI result, and Real User Journey Acceptance operates a browser surface with screenshot evidence. This is correct for a platform-completion product whose users operate the product directly, but incomplete for a product in which a customer's Personal Agent performs permitted work with the platform.

The Owner requires two changes:

1. Make LCCoding's applicability boundary explicit at the beginning of Initial.
2. Introduce an Agent-collaborative product service path alongside the traditional platform-completion path, explain both to the developer during Initial, and carry the selected service topology through Calabash, Product Formation, Real Product Integration, Real User Journey Acceptance, and Delivery Preparation.

## 2. Design decision

LCCoding 3.1 keeps one five-phase lifecycle. It does not split into a traditional edition and an Agent edition, add a sixth phase, create a second Backend/Core, or make every capability available through every channel.

The lifecycle becomes service-topology-aware:

```text
Owner Proposal
→ LCCoding Applicability Assessment
→ Proposal Readiness and Product Service Strategy Discussion
→ Project Initialization
→ INITIAL_READY
→ Calabash Draft with required Service Route Map
→ Simulation-first Product Formation
→ Mandatory Calabash Upgrade
→ Product Baseline
→ route-bound Feature Slices and Real Product Integration
→ route-faithful Real User Journey Acceptance
→ Delivery Preparation
→ Delivery
```

The two primary product service strategies are:

1. `PLATFORM_COMPLETION`: a person uses the product surface and the platform performs the principal business work.
2. `AGENT_COLLABORATIVE`: a person's external Personal Agent performs permitted edge work and collaborates with the platform to produce the business result.

A product may adopt both. This is not a third architecture or duplicate product; it is one shared capability system with more than one required service route.

When the intended market includes people without a suitable Personal Agent, the Agent-collaborative discussion covers an online or offline Service Center. The Service Center is an assisted service route and possible paid service, not a substitute user identity, a hidden impersonation path, or a separate Backend/Core.

## 3. Applicability boundary

### 3.1 Purpose

Applicability Assessment answers whether the full LCCoding lifecycle is a suitable method for the proposed engineering object. It does not decide whether the engineering work is valuable or technically possible, and a negative assessment does not stop the underlying project. It prevents the method from being forced onto an unsuitable object.

### 3.2 Assessment factors

The assessment considers whether the proposed scope has:

- an identifiable human beneficiary or an authorized representative acting for one;
- a complete product or service journey from intent to observable outcome;
- real business Workflow with actors, rules, state transitions, effects, and recovery;
- an actor-facing product surface, such as interactive UI, BI/result surface, Agent service surface, or assisted-service surface;
- a reason to connect product surface, Workflow, Simulation, and Backend/Core into a delivered product;
- a feasible real-journey acceptance path from an actual promised entry point.

It does not mechanically require a graphical browser UI. A CLI, API-first service, Personal Agent route, or operational console can be a real product surface when it is the actual customer experience and produces an observable business outcome.

### 3.3 Outcomes

The assessment produces one concise recommendation:

- `WHOLE_PRODUCT_FIT`: apply LCCoding to the proposed product scope.
- `BOUNDED_PRODUCT_FIT`: identify one complete product-facing scope and apply LCCoding there without absorbing unrelated infrastructure or components.
- `OTHER_METHOD_RECOMMENDED`: explain why the full lifecycle is unsuitable and recommend another engineering approach without treating the project itself as rejected.

Insufficient proposal information remains a Proposal Readiness problem; it is not silently converted into a negative applicability judgment.

## 4. Product service strategy discussion

After a whole-product or bounded-product fit, LCCoding actively explains the two service strategies to the developer instead of assuming that the developer already understands Agent-mediated product design.

The discussion is concrete:

| Question | Platform completion | Agent collaboration |
|---|---|---|
| Primary entry | Web/App or another direct human surface | External Personal Agent |
| Principal execution | Platform Workflow and Backend/Core | Personal Agent edge work plus platform Workflow and Backend/Core |
| Human surface | Full operational interaction | Results, BI, consent, confirmation, exception handling, and optional direct operation |
| Machine surface | Primarily internal UI-facing contracts | Discoverable Agent-facing capability, task, result, authorization, and error contracts |
| No-Agent user | Uses the direct product surface | Uses a direct route when offered or an authorized Service Center route |
| Main product risk | UI/Workflow/Core mismatch | Identity, delegation, authorization, route drift, non-deterministic interpretation, and result explainability |

LCCoding recommends a strategy from product facts and explains trade-offs. The Owner retains product meaning and commercial authority. The developer supplies feasibility, cost, security, operational, and user-experience consequences rather than merely asking the Owner to invent a technical solution.

The Initial decision records the primary strategy, any required coexisting strategy, intended user groups, the Service Center applicability decision, reasons, material uncertainties, and the Calabash topics that need further definition. It is an initial direction, not a claim that every capability already has a final route.

## 5. Calabash ownership and negotiation

Calabash owns the product meaning of the selected service topology. LCCoding owns when the discussion begins, how evidence returns to it, when the result is adopted, and how later impact is handled.

The Owner-developer discussion starts in Initial and continues through Calabash Draft and Product Formation. Simulation, Workflow, UI, Agent-service, security, operational, and commercial evidence may refine the initial direction. Mandatory Calabash Upgrade resolves or governs contradictions before Product Baseline.

Calabash records the following without creating a second LCCoding authority:

- human principal, direct user, Personal Agent, Product Agent, Operations Agent, Service Center operator, and Service Center Agent identities;
- delegation, consent, revocation, expiry, liability, privacy, escalation, and audit meaning;
- which work may occur at the edge and which state or decision remains platform-authoritative;
- product entry points and route transitions;
- commercial treatment and service obligations for the Service Center;
- per-journey required service routes;
- route-specific Snake risks and Scorpion hard blocks;
- evidence required to claim each route as delivered.

After Product Baseline, a material route or product-meaning change uses the existing governed Calabash return and impact path. It is not silently introduced by UI, Workflow, Agent, Runtime, or Delivery work.

## 6. One capability system, multiple routes

The architecture is one product capability system:

```text
Human Principal
├─ Direct Product Surface ───────────────┐
├─ Personal Agent ─ Agent Service Surface├─→ Workflow → Backend/Core → Business Result
└─ Service Center ─ Assisted Surface ────┘
```

The routes may have different interaction and presentation contracts, but they converge on the same product meaning, business rules, authoritative state, side effects, and outcome identity. A route adapter does not create duplicate business logic.

The Service Center may use a staff console, an authorized Service Center Agent, or both. Its acts remain attributable to the human principal, the staff or service Agent, the delegation basis, the performed action, and the resulting product state.

### 6.1 Service Route Map

Calabash Draft produces one Service Route Map as part of the existing product definition. It is not a new lifecycle phase or independent authority.

For every delivered CORE journey and every delivered EXTRA journey represented as product capability, the map states:

- actor and human beneficiary;
- required direct-product route, Personal Agent route, and Service Center route;
- unsupported or future routes that cannot be claimed as delivered;
- route entry, intended outcome, and permitted transitions;
- authority, consent, and confirmation boundary;
- shared capability and route-specific adapter/surface;
- acceptance evidence required for that route.

A project-level strategy does not imply that every action is available to an Agent. High-risk, irreversible, regulated, identity-sensitive, or meaning-ambiguous actions may require human confirmation or remain human-only. Fine-grained authorization is defined by action and resource rather than one broad Agent permission.

## 7. Product Formation changes

Product Formation retains Simulation-first construction and separately runnable product ends. It becomes route-aware.

For platform completion:

```text
Simulation foundation
→ Workflow capability end ∥ direct human product surface
```

For Agent collaboration:

```text
Simulation foundation
→ Workflow capability end
  ∥ Agent service surface
  ∥ human result/BI/consent/exception surface
  ∥ applicable Service Center surface
```

These surfaces do not create duplicate Workflow. API, MCP, A2A, or another protocol is selected from product and interoperability needs; LCCoding does not require a particular provider or protocol. Agent-facing contracts cover discoverable capability, structured request, task state, result/artifact, error, identity, authorization, idempotency, cancellation, recovery, and audit semantics where applicable.

Simulation models the selected actors and required route behavior, including authorization denial, revocation, Agent failure, ambiguous intent, partial completion, duplicate requests, channel switching, Service Center escalation, and final human comprehension. Depth remains proportional to actual product risk.

## 8. Real Product Integration changes

Feature Slice is generalized from a fixed UI action to a route-bound actor-visible claim:

```text
promised real entry
→ authenticated actor and valid authority
→ real route adapter or product surface
→ real Workflow
→ authoritative state/data/side effects
→ route result
→ human-observable business outcome
```

Platform-completion Slices continue to prove direct UI behavior. Agent-collaborative Slices start from a real or conformant external Personal Agent request and prove identity, delegation, task/result semantics, Workflow/Core effects, and the final result delivered to the human. Service Center Slices prove the assisted request, staff or Agent identity, delegation boundary, shared capability, audit lineage, and user delivery.

Each required route receives sufficient Slice evidence. Unaffected shared Workflow/Core evidence is reused across routes; route-specific adapters and user outcomes are not inferred from another route's PASS.

The existing one-way UI lock remains applicable to human-visible UI. It does not turn Agent-service or Service Center surfaces into permission to change locked UI. New route surfaces receive their own exact candidate and contract identity through existing baseline and impact mechanisms.

## 9. Real User Journey Acceptance changes

The phase name remains `REAL_USER_JOURNEY_ACCEPTANCE`. Its meaning becomes route-faithful rather than browser-exclusive.

The acceptance graph is derived from the adopted Service Route Map. Every route claimed as required and delivered begins at its actual external entry and ends when the human beneficiary receives and can understand the intended business outcome.

Evidence follows the real route:

| Route | First-hand acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| Direct Web/App | visible action, rendered feedback, screenshot, and final business outcome |
| Personal Agent | human goal, Agent identity, request/task messages, authorization events, result/artifact, platform effects, Agent response, and human-visible outcome |
| Service Center | user request, staff/Agent identity, delegation basis, assisted actions, platform result, communication to the user, and audit record |

Screenshots remain required after meaningful visible actions. They are not fabricated for non-visual protocol steps. Candidate-bound messages, task transitions, artifacts, authorization decisions, and audit events are first-hand evidence for an Agent route rather than merely diagnostic substitutes. The final result must still be observable by the human beneficiary.

The existing `40001+` defect identity, correction history, dependent-route continuation, complete-round restart, and concise Owner verdict remain. A complete round covers every currently required delivered route, not every imaginable channel.

The normal correction order becomes:

```text
user-facing service boundary → Workflow/orchestration → Backend/Core
```

For a direct route, the service boundary is UI. For an Agent route, it may be capability discovery, request/authorization/task/result behavior, or the final human-facing Agent response. For a Service Center route, it may be the assisted surface or user communication. Root-cause evidence may still justify one coherent cross-layer correction.

## 10. Delivery Preparation changes

Delivery protects what the Product Baseline and Phase-4 acceptance actually claim. In addition to existing security and package evidence, an Agent-collaborative delivery includes the applicable Agent discovery/capability contract, authorization and credential decisions, supported task/result behavior, recovery and revocation instructions, Service Center operating responsibility, and customer-facing route limitations.

Unsupported and future routes remain visibly unclaimed. A Delivery package does not imply Agent compatibility merely because API or MCP endpoints exist.

## 11. Identity and authorization boundary

LCCoding 3.1 distinguishes five identities:

1. Human Principal: the person whose intent, data, rights, and outcome are involved.
2. Personal Agent: an external customer-controlled representative.
3. Product Agent: an internal delivered Agent participating in product business behavior.
4. Operations Agent: an internal delivered Agent responsible for operation and maintenance.
5. Service Center actor: an authorized staff member or Service Center Agent assisting a human principal.

No role name, prompt switch, shared memory namespace, or bearer token alone proves delegation. Product policy determines when the Personal Agent may act independently, when step-up authorization or human confirmation is needed, and when the action is unavailable through that route.

The design is protocol-neutral but consistent with current interoperability direction: A2A separates Agent discovery, identity/capability metadata, tasks, messages, artifacts, and authentication; MCP authorization binds tokens to intended resources and supports incremental scopes; OAuth Rich Authorization Requests supports action- and resource-specific authorization details. These are engineering references, not mandatory product dependencies.

## 12. Compatibility and migration

LCCoding 3.1 is a method-schema change, not a sixth lifecycle phase. Existing 3.0 projects retain their accepted evidence. Migration is copy-on-write and evidence-conservative:

- no existing browser journey becomes an Agent-route PASS;
- no API/MCP existence becomes proof that an Agent-collaborative route is delivered;
- an existing direct-product project may migrate as `PLATFORM_COMPLETION` when its accepted product facts support that classification;
- Agent or Service Center routes begin unproved until their Service Route Map, integration, and journey evidence exist;
- a project that is not an LCCoding fit is not retroactively invalidated; the assessment informs future method use and scope decisions.

The built-in BI receives only the minimum schema projection necessary to show applicability and service-route summaries without becoming a route editor, control plane, evidence browser, or product surface. No visual redesign is part of this change.

## 13. Mechanical authority and validation

The active implementation should add the smallest coherent authority surface:

- one applicability and service-topology normative clause or a tightly grouped pair;
- focused reference guidance for Initial/service strategy and route-aware acceptance where conditional detail would otherwise bloat `SKILL.md`;
- schema-selected contracts and templates for the applicability result and Service Route Map summary;
- validators that reject fabricated route evidence while preserving supported 3.0 reads;
- migration from 3.0 to 3.1 that does not manufacture Agent-route acceptance;
- focused tests for applicability, route mapping, route-bound Slice semantics, route-faithful acceptance, identity separation, compatibility, and BI projection;
- version, manifest, release, and hash updates only when the implementation candidate is closed.

Tests should verify observable invariants, not merely exact prose. Important negative cases include forcing LCCoding onto a non-product scope, claiming all actions from a project-level Agent strategy, treating API/MCP presence as delivered Agent behavior, accepting a Personal Agent as a Product Agent, using a Service Center without attributable delegation, substituting browser evidence for an Agent route, and substituting Agent logs for the final human-observable outcome.

## 14. Non-goals

- No new lifecycle phase, construction role, execution method, Runtime, protocol, or Agent provider.
- No separate Agent edition of LCCoding.
- No second Workflow or Backend/Core for Personal Agent or Service Center traffic.
- No requirement that every product, journey, action, or user adopt a Personal Agent.
- No claim that every API-first or headless component is an LCCoding product.
- No forced graphical UI for a real Agent-first product.
- No weakening of Owner product authority, UI lock, security closure, or Delivery protection.
- No BI visual redesign, persistent BI deployment, global Skill deployment, tag, or Release in the implementation plan unless the Owner separately requests release work.
