# Product Service Strategy and Topology

Load this reference only for Initial strategy discussion, route-aware Product Formation, or route-aware Real User Journey Acceptance.

## Initial strategy discussion

Explain both primary strategies before recommending one:

- `PLATFORM_COMPLETION`: a person uses a direct product surface and the platform performs the principal business work.
- `AGENT_COLLABORATIVE`: a person's external Personal Agent performs permitted edge work and collaborates with the platform Workflow and Backend/Core.

`MIXED` means both primary strategies are required in one product. It is a summary of coexistence, not a third architecture. Record the selected primary strategy, any required coexisting strategy, intended user groups, feasibility and cost consequences, security and operational consequences, user-experience trade-offs, rationale, material uncertainties, and unresolved Calabash topics.

## Service Center coverage

When intended users may lack a suitable Personal Agent, discuss whether an online or offline Service Center is applicable. It is an authorized assisted-service route and possible paid service. It must not become a substitute identity, hidden impersonation path, or separate capability implementation.

## Formation and acceptance refinement

The Initial choice is a direction for Calabash, not blanket permission for every action or a claim that a route is delivered. Calabash refines required, unsupported, and future routes per delivered journey, including consent, confirmation, revocation, escalation, audit, commercial meaning, and route-specific evidence.

Keep Human Principal, Personal Agent, Product Agent, Operations Agent, and Service Center actor identities distinct. Direct-product, Personal-Agent, and Service-Center adapters may differ, but all routes converge on one shared Workflow and Backend/Core, authoritative state, business rules, side effects, and outcome identity.

During route-aware Product Formation, use the adopted Calabash direction to shape the Service Route Map and required surfaces without duplicating business logic. During route-aware Real User Journey Acceptance, start each required delivered route at its real promised entry and finish with a human-observable outcome; do not infer one route's delivery from another route's evidence.
